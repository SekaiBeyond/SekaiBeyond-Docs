---
title: Sign in with Sekai Beyond
description: Let people sign in to your Firebase app with their Sekai Beyond account, and learn who they are.
---

Registered apps such as SKBY Manager can sign people in with their Sekai Beyond accounts. Instead of sharing a database, `issueAppToken` mints a Firebase custom token **for the app's own Firebase project**, signed through IAM as the app's service account, so no service-account key file is ever stored.

- **The main site** tells the app who someone is. That's all a sign-in reads: the *site's* data, such as events and the team, is read with the app's [API keys](./keys.mdx), from its server, as far as each key's scopes go.
- **The app** keeps its own data, and decides who may do what in the app, and who of its users sees what its keys read, in its own database and rules.

Any signed-in account here may sign in to any registered app that has user sign-in enabled, so the app decides who may use it: see [Deciding who may use your app](#deciding-who-may-use-your-app).

<Steps>
<Step>

### Register the app

In **Tools > Apps**, register the app with **People sign in to it with their account here** ticked:

- **Redirect URIs**: 1–10 URLs where sign-in may hand people back to (`https://`, or `http://localhost` / `http://127.0.0.1` for local testing, with no `#fragment`). Pages on those origins may also call [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) from a browser (CORS).
- **Firebase project ID** and **Service account**: the app's own Firebase project ID (such as `skby-manager`) and the service account its custom tokens are signed as (usually its `firebase-adminsdk` service account).

</Step>
<Step>

### Grant IAM roles on the app's project

Open **Setup for its maintainers** on the app's card in **Tools > Apps** and run the two `gcloud` commands shown there once on the app's project:

1. `roles/iam.serviceAccountTokenCreator` on the app's service account, so our functions can sign custom tokens for the app's project.
2. `roles/firebaseauth.admin` on the app's project, so signing out everywhere, changing a password, undoing an email change, or deleting an account here also revokes or deletes that user in the app's project.

Sign-in needs the first: until it's granted, **Continue** on `/authorize` fails. Without the second, sign-in works, but the app's own project is never told when someone's sign-ins end here.

</Step>
<Step>

### Send the person to `/authorize`

Redirect the browser to:

```
https://sekaibeyond.com/authorize?app=<appId>&state=<state>&redirect_uri=<redirectUri>
```

- `app`: the app's ID.
- `state`: 16–128 characters of `A-Z`, `a-z`, `0-9`, `_` and `-`, generated randomly for this sign-in and kept in `sessionStorage` so you can check it when they return.
- `redirect_uri`: optional when you want the first redirect URI registered on the app; when given, it must match one of the app's registered redirect URIs.

Someone who isn't signed in here yet is asked to sign in first. The page then shows the app's name, its maintainers (if any), and what it will see: their name, photo and role here. It never shares their email or password. Nothing happens until they click **Continue**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Finish signing in and ask who they are

When they click **Continue**, `/authorize` redirects to:

```
<redirect_uri>#token=<customToken>&state=<state>
```

The token is in the URL fragment (`#…`), so it never reaches a server in a request URL or `Referer` header. In your app:

1. Read `token` and `state` from `window.location.hash`, clear the hash with `history.replaceState`, and verify that `state` matches what you sent.
2. Call `signInWithCustomToken(auth, token)` on your app's Firebase Auth instance. The user now has the same `uid` in your project as on `sekaibeyond.com`.
3. To learn who they are here, call [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) from the browser (on a registered origin, at `https://sekaibeyond.com/api/v1/me`) or from your server (`https://sekaibeyond.com/api/v1/me` or `https://api.sekaibeyond.com/v1/me`), with the user's Firebase ID token (`await user.getIdToken()`) in the `Authorization` header:

```js
const idToken = await auth.currentUser.getIdToken();
const res = await fetch('https://sekaibeyond.com/api/v1/me', {
  headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${idToken}` },
});
const me = await res.json();
```

Every other endpoint needs one of the app's API keys: a sign-in answers them with `403 api-key-required`. See [Reading the site's data](#reading-the-sites-data).

</Step>
</Steps>

## Token claims and live checks

The custom token's claims carry onto every ID token the sign-in renews to:

- `app`: the app's ID (`appId`).
- `group`: the person's group here (`president`, `core-staff`, `staff` or `user`).
- `mintedAt`: when the sign-in was issued, in milliseconds since the epoch.

The claims are as of the sign-in. When the person's group changes, they're signed out of your app, so their next sign-in carries the new one: see below.

Every call with a sign-in re-reads the person's account here:

- **Signing out everywhere, signing out other devices, setting a new password (changed or reset), or undoing an email change** immediately causes `/api/v1` to refuse tokens from before that moment with `401 session-ended`. It also revokes the person's refresh tokens in your app's Firebase project, so their sign-in there ends once its current ID token expires, within the hour.
- **Deleting an account** takes effect when the account is removed, at least 48 hours after deletion is requested, unless that's cancelled. From then `/api/v1` answers `401 session-ended`, and the user is deleted from your app's Firebase project.
- **Changing a person's group** signs them out of every app. Their refresh tokens in your app's project are revoked: their sign-in there ends once its current ID token expires, within the hour, and they sign in again with the new group. `/v1/me` returns the change at once, and `/api/v1` keeps accepting their current sign-in. Changing only their title, or which events they staff, changes no claim and signs no one out.
- **Turning off an app's sign-in or removing the app** reaches `/api/v1` within 10 seconds.
- Each person may make **60 calls a minute** per app.

Revoking and deleting in your app's project need the second IAM role above. `/api/v1` refuses the sign-in either way.

## Deciding who may use your app

Anyone with an account here can sign in to your app. A sign-in says who someone is, not that they may use the app. Decide that in your own project, such as with an access list in your database that your security rules check:

```
function signedInWithSekaiBeyond() {
  return request.auth != null
    && request.auth.token.firebase.sign_in_provider == 'custom'
    && request.auth.token.app == '<appId>';
}

match /projects/{projectId} {
  allow read, write: if signedInWithSekaiBeyond()
    && exists(/databases/$(database)/documents/projects/$(projectId)/access/$(request.auth.uid));
}
```

Rules can also go by the person's group here, from the token's `group` claim, and by what your server has copied into your database with the app's keys, such as each event's staff:

```
function signedInWithSekaiBeyond() {
  return request.auth != null
    && request.auth.token.firebase.sign_in_provider == 'custom'
    && request.auth.token.app == '<appId>';
}

// Used within 5 minutes of being issued, as /authorize's sign-ins are. One held back
// for longer could carry claims from before a change it was never signed out for.
function usedPromptly() {
  return request.auth.token.auth_time * 1000 - request.auth.token.mintedAt < 300000;
}

// Core staff and the president here.
function isCoreStaff() {
  return signedInWithSekaiBeyond() && usedPromptly()
    && request.auth.token.group in ['core-staff', 'president'];
}

// Staff of an event here, while it runs. Rules can't read the site, so your server
// copies each event's start and end (GET /v1/events) and its staff
// (GET /v1/events/{eventId}/staff) into your own database with the app's key.
function staffsDuring(eventId) {
  let event = get(/databases/$(database)/documents/events/$(eventId)).data;
  return signedInWithSekaiBeyond()
    && exists(/databases/$(database)/documents/events/$(eventId)/staff/$(request.auth.uid))
    && request.time >= event.startAt
    && request.time < event.endAt;
}

match /events/{eventId}/checklists/{checklistId} {
  allow read: if isCoreStaff() || staffsDuring(eventId);
}
```

- **Check `app` as well as the provider,** so only a sign-in issued for your app counts.
- **Check that the sign-in was used promptly** (`usedPromptly`) wherever you check `group`. A sign-in token works for an hour once issued, so one issued before a change and used after it would start a sign-in with the old group that no sign-out reaches.
- **`group` can be up to an hour out of date.** A change signs the person out of your app, but their current ID token still works until it expires, and rules can't tell it's been revoked. Where that hour matters, such as taking access away at once, keep your own access list, or check [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) from your server.
- **Without `roles/firebaseauth.admin` on your project** (see above), a change never signs anyone out of your app, so `group` stays as it was until the person signs in again. Don't check it in rules without that role.
- **What your server copies is as fresh as it keeps it.** Changing who staffs an event signs no one out, so copy it again as often as your rules need, such as on a schedule and just before an event.

## Reading the site's data

A sign-in reaches only [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx). Events, the team, an event's staff and orders are read with an [API key](./keys.mdx) under the app, from your server, as far as the key's scopes go. A key reads the same whoever is using your app, unpublished events included, so your app decides who of its users sees what, such as by the sign-in's `group` or your own access list.
