---
title: Sign in with Sekai Beyond
description: Let people sign in to your Firebase app with their Sekai Beyond account, and call the API on their behalf.
---

Registered apps such as SKBY Manager can sign people in with their Sekai Beyond accounts. Instead of sharing a database, `issueAppToken` mints a Firebase custom token **for the app's own Firebase project**, signed through IAM as the app's service account, so no service-account key file is ever stored.

- **The main site** tells the app who someone is, and decides what of the *site's* data that person can read through the app.
- **The app** keeps its own data, and decides who may do what in the app, in its own database and rules.

Any signed-in account here may sign in to any registered app that has user sign-in enabled, so the app decides who may use it: see [Deciding who may use your app](#deciding-who-may-use-your-app).

<Steps>
<Step>

### Register the app

In **Tools > Apps**, register the app with **People sign in to it with their account here** ticked:

- **Redirect URIs**: 1–10 URLs where sign-in may hand people back to (`https://`, or `http://localhost` / `http://127.0.0.1` for local testing, with no `#fragment`). Pages on those origins may also call `/api/v1` from a browser (CORS).
- **What it may read for the person signed in** (optional): `events:read`, `team:read`, `event-staff:read`. Every app gets the person's name, photo and group via [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) without any scope.
- **Firebase project ID** and **Service account**: the app's own Firebase project ID (such as `skby-manager`) and the service account its custom tokens are signed as (usually its `firebase-adminsdk` service account).

</Step>
<Step>

### Grant IAM roles on the app's project

Open **Setup for its owner** on the app's card in **Tools > Apps** and run the two `gcloud` commands shown there once on the app's project:

1. `roles/iam.serviceAccountTokenCreator` on the app's service account, so our functions can sign custom tokens for the app's project.
2. `roles/firebaseauth.admin` on the app's project, so signing out everywhere, changing a password, undoing an email change, or deleting an account here also revokes or deletes that user in the app's project.

Sign-in needs the first: until it's granted, **Continue** on `/authorize` fails. Without the second, sign-in works, but the app's own project is never told when someone's sign-ins end here.

</Step>
<Step>

### Send the person to `/authorize`

Redirect the browser to:

```
https://sekaibeyond.com/authorize?app=<appId>&state=<state>&redirect_uri=<redirectUri>
```

- `app`: the app's ID.
- `state`: 16–128 characters of `A-Z`, `a-z`, `0-9`, `_` and `-`, generated randomly for this sign-in and kept in `sessionStorage` so you can check it when they return.
- `redirect_uri`: optional when you want the first redirect URI registered on the app; when given, it must match one of the app's registered redirect URIs.

Someone who isn't signed in here yet is asked to sign in first. The page then shows the app's name, its owner (if set), and what it will see: their name, photo and role here, plus one line per scope. It never shares their email or password. Nothing happens until they click **Continue**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Finish signing in and call `/api/v1`

When they click **Continue**, `/authorize` redirects to:

```
<redirect_uri>#token=<customToken>&state=<state>
```

The token is in the URL fragment (`#…`), so it never reaches a server in a request URL or `Referer` header. In your app:

1. Read `token` and `state` from `window.location.hash`, clear the hash with `history.replaceState`, and verify that `state` matches what you sent.
2. Call `signInWithCustomToken(auth, token)` on your app's Firebase Auth instance. The user now has the same `uid` in your project as on `sekaibeyond.com`.
3. To call `/api/v1` from the browser (on a registered origin, at `https://sekaibeyond.com/api/v1`) or from your server (`https://sekaibeyond.com/api/v1` or `https://api.sekaibeyond.com/v1`), pass the user's Firebase ID token (`await user.getIdToken()`) in the `Authorization` header:

```js
const idToken = await auth.currentUser.getIdToken();
const res = await fetch('https://sekaibeyond.com/api/v1/me', {
  headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${idToken}` },
});
const me = await res.json();
```

</Step>
</Steps>

## Token claims and live checks

The custom token's claims carry onto every ID token the sign-in renews to:

- `app`: the app's ID (`appId`).
- `group`: the person's group here when they signed in (`president`, `core-staff`, `staff` or `user`). Read [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) for their current group and profile.
- `mintedAt`: when the sign-in was issued, in milliseconds since the epoch.

Every `/api/v1` call re-reads the person's account here:

- **Signing out everywhere, signing out other devices, setting a new password (changed or reset), or undoing an email change** immediately causes `/api/v1` to refuse tokens from before that moment with `401 session-ended`. It also revokes the person's refresh tokens in your app's Firebase project, so their sign-in there ends once its current ID token expires, within the hour.
- **Deleting an account** takes effect when the account is removed, at least 48 hours after deletion is requested, unless that's cancelled. From then `/api/v1` answers `401 session-ended`, and the user is deleted from your app's Firebase project.
- **Changing a person's group** does not sign them out; `/v1/me` returns the new group at once.
- **Changing an app's scopes or removing the app** reaches `/api/v1` within 10 seconds.
- Each person may make **60 calls a minute** per app.

Revoking and deleting in your app's project need the second IAM role above. `/api/v1` refuses the sign-in either way.

## Deciding who may use your app

Anyone with an account here can sign in to your app. A sign-in says who someone is, not that they may use the app. Decide that in your own project, such as with an access list in your database that your security rules check:

```
function signedInWithSekaiBeyond() {
  return request.auth != null
    && request.auth.token.firebase.sign_in_provider == 'custom'
    && request.auth.token.app == '<appId>';
}

match /projects/{projectId} {
  allow read, write: if signedInWithSekaiBeyond()
    && exists(/databases/$(database)/documents/projects/$(projectId)/access/$(request.auth.uid));
}
```

- **Check `app` as well as the provider,** so only a sign-in issued for your app counts.
- **Don't rely on the token's `group` alone.** It's the person's group when they signed in, and it stays that way when their group here changes. Rules that check only it keep letting someone in after they've lost that group, until they sign in again. Read [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) for their group as it is now.

## Person scopes

| Scope | Lets the app |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Call [`GET /v1/me`](../api-reference/get-me.mdx) for the signed-in person |
| `events:read` | [List events](../api-reference/list-events.mdx) and [get an event](../api-reference/get-event.mdx) that the person can see on the site |
| `team:read` | [List the team](../api-reference/list-team.mdx), if the person signed in is staff or above |
| `event-staff:read` | [List an event's staff](../api-reference/list-event-staff.mdx), if the person signed in is staff or that event's staff |
